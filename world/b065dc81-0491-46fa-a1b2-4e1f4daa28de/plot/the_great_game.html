The thread, so far

  1. the twelfth day after the first frost, the hour of the second bell · Rising
    The Ball at the End of the World

    Valdris signed the Ball writs in his own blood while Selene read and withheld the Iron Vanguard's offer. Neither spoke of it. The writs go out by evening; the northern letter stays in Selene's sleeve.

  2. the twenty-second morning after the Feast of Lanterns · Quiet Hour
    The Letter Selene Did Not Burn

    Valdris produced the Iron Vanguard's intercepted offer and left it between them, unanswered. Selene neither claimed nor denied it. Both waited.

  3. the ninth evening before the Feast of Lanterns ends · Rising
    The Writ and the Cough

    Selene deflects the Emperor's question about the Ball; neither signs the writ, and Valdris notices her steadiness has replaced the tremor that defined her a month ago.

  4. the ninth evening after the Feast of Lanterns · Quiet Hour
    The Unsigned Writ

    Valdris refuses to sign the Ball writ until Selene reveals what she has hidden: Sera Vyne found the Tuning Fork nine days ago and is deciding which lord to sell it to.

  5. the twenty-third night, the hour of wolves · Quiet Hour
    What the Cantor Does Not Say

    Selene confesses to Valdris that she let Sera Vyne's ship sail deliberately, and that she has known his remaining lifespan for some time. Valdris presses her on what she wants him to do with the truth.

  6. the twenty-fourth dawn, the hour the frost holds · Complication
    The Emperor Signs Nothing

    Emperor Valdris confronted Selene about her nine-day silence and refused to sign the writ to march on the Thornfield. Selene argued that the Red Octave would arrive first and that the Emperor must choose between trusting her silence or his physician's counsel.

  7. the twenty-sixth dawn, before the bells · Complication
    What the Cantor Has Done

    Confessor Varn's dispatch confirmed the Chorus holds the heir; Sera Vyne refused to bring him south. Cantor Selene delivered the Chorus's invitation in person — a leash dressed as hospitality. Valdris accepted the ball, refused to sign the writ, and reminded Selene that his unsigned silence remains his only weapon.

  8. the thirty-fifth dawn, the hour the forge remembers it was born · Complication
    What the Cantor Does Not Send

    Cantor Selene laid three gifts before Emperor Valdris and withheld the fourth: that she has already decided not to send word to Bastion. Valdris refused to sign until she named the silence. She named it. Now neither the heir nor the Red Octave will hear from the Cantor's office today, and Valdris must decide whether to let her hold that silence or break it himself.

  9. the fortieth dawn, the hour the forge remembers it was born · Quiet Hour
    What the Cantor Keeps in the Quiet

    Valdris and Selene meet privately. Valdris reveals the Iron Vanguard's grey feather — the Red Octave is close to the boy. Selene explains the Red Octave will hollow the boy, not kill him, and only Bastion's timely arrival can preserve his usefulness.

  10. the forty-second dawn, the hour the frost holds its breath · Complication
    The Count's Gambit

    Valdris placed a signed heir's writ in Selene's hand at the Great Ball, revealing the Crimson Weave have already been authorized to act, and gave her one hour before the boy's hollowing to countermand them. The writ binds whichever heir she names to the Crown's succession.

  11. the forty-fourth evening, the hour the ice takes the longest to crack · Rising
    What the Silence Signs

    Cantor Selene confronted Confessor Varn about the doctrine's silence around the dying Emperor. Varn broke twenty years of confessional distance to tell her the heir is not a weapon — he is mercy. Selene took the writ into her sleeve, not signed, not destroyed.
